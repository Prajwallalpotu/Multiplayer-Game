# Six-human playtest guide

Do not show players the story bible or puzzle solution document. Use six humans who have not read the episode, six ordinary laptops, six phones, headphones, an HTTPS origin and TURN. Assign an observer who does not supply answers. Play a casual session first.

## Before play

Record browser/OS, phone model, screen size, network (home Wi-Fi versus cellular), accessibility settings and familiarity with cooperative mysteries. Check each QR controls only its own laptop. Each participant should speak, mute, hear every other participant and adjust one individual volume. Include one person using text fallback and one using text equivalents for audio evidence. Avoid capturing conversations without consent; written timing observations are enough.

## Record meaningful observations

- Pairing/setup duration; every point that requires the facilitator.
- Time to first private observation, first shared clue, first useful conversation, first deduction.
- Every puzzle: start/end, incorrect explanation, hints (1/2/3), discoveries that felt unfair or over-explicit.
- Per role: useful observations, reasoning contributions, walking versus thinking time, longest idle stretch. Flag an idle stretch over three minutes and determine whether parallel leads or UI caused it.
- Time spent looking at phone versus laptop. Can players choose answers, consult clues, navigate dialogs, send messages and complete the finale with phones alone?
- Whether the correction, Voss identity, motive and restart hazard were inferred before explicit confirmations. Do not tell participants the twist.
- Which resident they care about, and whether the ending changes that feeling.
- Confusion versus productive disagreement. Which questions are answered too directly by a single clue? Which deduction interfaces feel repetitive?
- Total active and wall-clock duration. **90 minutes is unverified**. If short, deepen reasoning and environmental discovery; do not add delays or repetitive locks.

## Recovery and fairness drills

1. Background a phone while walking. Character must stop within the watchdog window; restore the page and retry voice if needed.
2. Refresh a laptop during a deduction. Confirm its phone link, private observations and selected choices survive.
3. Disconnect a phone for 60 seconds. Team pauses; no other investigator inherits the seat. Replace the phone using the laptop’s settings pairing flow.
4. Restart the server during Act III. Seats, evidence, puzzle completions, hint counts and selected drafts persist. All devices reconnect; no duplicate rewards.
5. Try a restart in Act IV. The safety trip explains the new capture subjects, preserves progress, and permits reversal.
6. In the finale, arm a station, disconnect its player, reconnect and continue. No synchronized button press or timing window should be required.
7. Confirm ranked time includes all pauses/downtime while casual active time stops. Verify assisted/unassisted categories and no invented standings.
8. Test iOS Safari and Android Chrome on different networks with TURN forced via relay-only policy in a dedicated test build. Verify microphone denial, playback gesture, muted rejoin, PTT release on blur, wired/Bluetooth headphones and lock-screen behavior. Do not infer remote success from local peers.

## Debrief

Ask each player privately: “What did you contribute that nobody else could?”, “What conclusion did you help the group reach?”, “When did you stop trusting the radio?”, “Why would restart trap you?”, “Why could reversal work?”, and “Who returned that mattered to you?”

A fair mystery should produce compatible explanations of the rules without the facilitator. Revise authoring if one role only presses buttons or if clues narrate the entire solution before the group reasons. Follow the earliest confusion to its cause. Repeat with fresh players after content revisions and increment the episode version when changing ranked difficulty.

## Smaller groups and optional phones

For the updated Casual lobby, invite 1–6 people, claim multiple roles or use **Distribute unclaimed roles**, and confirm equipment on the selected controller. A laptop-only player chooses voice or text and confirms the keyboard check; no phone is needed. Phone players drag the joystick and complete their check there. All present players must be ready, with every specialty assigned.

During play, check that **Your next step** makes it clear where to investigate and which tool to use. Try **Read aloud** and **Spoken guidance** on your actual browsers. Switch specialties inside observations or via the toolbar. Walk toward a labeled doorway and use **Enter** rather than relying only on the map. Each finale latch remains set while you change specialties and travel to another station.

Also test a mixed laptop/phone voice group on different networks, a selected phone disconnect followed by switching to laptop in Settings, joystick drag/release and app backgrounding, and an open public directory while a host disconnects or starts the case. Existing saved cases keep their original controller preference.

## Interactive trail and narration regression

- In the archive, approach **The scrambled picture**. Restore its four tiles, then check that the combination appears in every player's **Team inventory**.
- In the clinic, approach **The locked cupboard**. Try a wrong combination, then use the recovered one; look for the brass winding key reward.
- In the clock tower, approach **Inez’s music box**. Insert the key and connect all nine air channels, from the upper-left inlet to the lower-right outlet. Check the visual reward and **Play Inez’s morning melody**. Complete this trail before or after the main case; it is optional.
- Click the logo to return home, then resume from the same browser. Inventory and puzzle progress should remain. The paired phone should also return home and stop its microphone. Test **Save & exit for everyone** from the host; all teammates return home and the case remains paused for a later session.
- Enable spoken guidance after the initial objective is already on screen. While it reads, have a teammate share evidence. The original reading should finish, followed by the latest objective; it should not restart every sentence. Try a manual transcript, **Stop reading**, and toggling guidance off. Real browser voices, especially Safari/iOS, still need listening checks.
